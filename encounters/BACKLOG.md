## Candidates for next encounter

Ranked by how much new thematic ground they cover (not already represented above).

### High priority — fills a real gap

- **The Emmaus Disciples** (Lucas 24:13-35)
  Theme: grief/confusion after the crucifixion, slow recognition, revelation in the
  ordinary (breaking bread). Distinct from Thomas (doubt resolved by physical proof) —
  here recognition comes through ordinary action, not evidence.

- **Peter's Restoration at the Shore** (Juan 21:1-19)
  Theme: guilt after failure, restored calling ("¿me amas?"), three questions answering
  three denials. Strong complement to peter_water_001 (fear/faith) with a different arc
  (failure/restoration). Only risk: two Peter encounters back-to-back in the series.

### Medium priority — solid, some thematic overlap

- **The Widow of Nain** (Lucas 7:11-17)
  Theme: grief, compassion ("se compadió de ella"), resurrection of a son. Compassion as
  the driver (not faith or persistence) is new; slight overlap with Mary Magdalene (grief)
  and gadarene_demoniac (resurrection-adjacent restoration).

- **The Prodigal Son** (Lucas 15:11-32)
  Theme: repentance, the father's running welcome, restoration of sonship. It's a parable,
  not a historical encounter — would need to decide whether the "character" is the son,
  the father, or both. Different format precedent to establish first.

- **The Centurion's Servant** (Mateo 8:5-13 / Lucas 7:1-10)
  Theme: faith without needing to see, humility of an outsider ("no soy digno"), authority
  recognized across cultures. Some overlap with Gentile-inclusion themes already touched
  in gadarene_demoniac.

### Lower priority — good stories, heavier overlap

- **Mary and Martha** (Lucas 10:38-42) — overlaps with Martha/Mary's brother Lazarus arc;
  "the one thing needful" theme not yet covered, but low dramatic tension for the format.
- **The Rich Young Ruler** (Marcos 10:17-27) — overlaps with Zacchaeus (wealth, cost of
  following); ends unresolved (he walks away sad), which breaks the series' pattern of
  restoration by the final card.
