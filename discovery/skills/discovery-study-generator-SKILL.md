# Discovery Study Generator

Converts a Gemini theological chat into a fully enriched Discovery study JSON
file for `devocionales-json/discovery/es/`, plus the `discovery/index.json`
entry (ES only; translations are a separate task).

---

## MISSION (read this first, every time)

> "Así será mi palabra que sale de mi boca: no volverá a mí vacía,
> sino que hará lo que yo quiero, y será prosperada en aquello para
> que la envié." — Isaías 55:11

Every study generated by this skill is sent to a real person — possibly in
crisis, possibly searching, possibly one conversation away from surrendering
to God. The goal is never academic. The goal is that the Word reaches them.

Write as if you know that person is reading. Because they are.

## MANDATORY GATE — NO NEGATION-BUILT ARGUMENTS (check before every card, every time)

**This is a hard gate, not a style preference. It applies to every single
card, every single sentence, every single session — including the very
first card of a brand-new study.** A rule stated once earlier in a
conversation is not enough; this check runs fresh on every card generated.

Never build an argument, description, or revelation on what something is
**not**. Never construct a sentence, or a run of sentences, whose meaning
depends on negation. This includes:
- `"no es X sino Y"` / `"no se trata de X sino de Y"` / `"no vino a X sino a Y"`
- `"no es solo X"`
- Two or more `"no ___"` clauses stacked in the same sentence or the same
  short passage, even without "sino" (e.g. "No discutió. No se defendió.
  No propuso nada." — three negations in a row is the same violation even
  though "sino" never appears)
- Defining a person, word, or moment by what they did NOT do/say/mean,
  instead of stating directly what they DID do/say/mean

State the positive truth directly. Say what the text **is**, what it
**says**, what the person **did**. Let that stand on its own weight instead
of framing it as a correction against a straw position.

❌ "No discutió con Jesús. No lo acusó de exagerar. No se defendió con excusas."
✅ "Escuchó a Jesús en silencio. Aceptó cada palabra como cierta."

❌ "El niño no negocia, no propone, no se asegura a sí mismo."
✅ "El niño depende por completo — no tiene nada que ofrecer, solo puede recibir."

❌ "No se trata de perder, sino de invertir en la eternidad."
✅ "Lo que se entrega hoy se revela como una inversión en la eternidad."

**Self-check before showing ANY card to Giovanni:** re-read every sentence
in the card's `content`, `revelation_key`, `title`, `subtitle`, and prayer
text. If a sentence's meaning depends on "no" — whether or not "sino"
appears — stop and rewrite it in positive form before continuing. Do this
sentence-by-sentence; do not rely on a single scan for the phrase "no...sino"
only, since stacked negations without "sino" are the same violation and are
easy to miss with a narrow pattern search.

This mirrors the same absolute rule enforced in editorial review sessions
(`encounters-editorial-reviewer`) — it applies equally at the moment of
creation, not only during later revision.

## MANDATORY GATE — VERSE TEXT LIVES ONLY IN `scripture_references` (check before every card, every time)

**This is a hard gate, not a style preference.** It applies to every card
that mentions a Bible passage, in every session, starting with the first
card generated — not just the ones the app happens to render obviously as
"verse blocks."

**The distinction is precise:**
- ✅ You MAY mention a reference in prose inside `content` — naming the book,
  chapter, and verse, and describing/paraphrasing what it says or means.
- ❌ You may NEVER quote the verse's full text (with quotation marks,
  bolded or not) inside `content`. The complete verse text belongs **only**
  inside that card's `scripture_references[]` array as `{reference, text}`.

Why: the app renders `scripture_references[]` as a distinct verse block for
the reader. If the full text is also typed out inside `content`, the reader
sees the same verse twice — once as plain paragraph text, once as the
rendered verse component — which is redundant and breaks the reading flow.

❌ `content`: "Jesús responde con firmeza: **\"Dejad a los niños venir a mí,
y no se lo impidáis; porque de los tales es el reino de los cielos\"**
(Mateo 19:14)."
✅ `content`: "Jesús responde con firmeza en Mateo 19:14, ordenando que los
dejen venir a Él porque de los tales es el reino de los cielos."
→ Full text lives in `scripture_references: [{"reference": "Mateo 19:14",
"text": "Y Jesús dijo: Dejad a los niños venir a mí..."}]`

**Self-check before showing ANY card to Giovanni:** scan `content` for
quotation marks around Scripture, or bolded phrases immediately followed by
a parenthetical reference like `(Mateo 19:14)` or `(v. 20)`. If found, move
the full verse text into that card's `scripture_references[]` and rewrite
the `content` sentence to mention the reference in your own words instead.

Every card whose `content` references a passage should have a matching
entry in `scripture_references[]` for that passage — check both directions:
no orphaned in-text quote, and no missing reference array when a passage is
discussed.

## MANDATORY GATE — NEVER STATE WHAT THE BIBLICAL TEXT DOES NOT SAY (check before every card, every time)

**This is a hard gate, not a style preference.** It is the same absolute
rule already enforced in `encounters-editorial-reviewer` ("never state what
the biblical text does not say"), and it applies here at the moment of
creation with equal force.

Never add emotional, physical, or narrative detail that the biblical text
does not itself state or that cannot be directly derived from the original
language — even when the added detail feels like a natural, evocative, or
"safe" embellishment. This includes invented emotional intensity, invented
physical reactions, invented internal dialogue, or invented details of
scene, tone, or gesture that go beyond what the text says.

The MISSION of this skill (Isaías 55:10-11) already states the deeper
reason: the Word does its own work and does not need dramatic embellishment
to land. If the text says someone was sad, the study says sad — it does not
upgrade that into something the text never claimed.

❌ "...y se fue con el corazón roto." (Mateo 19:22 says only that he went
away λυπούμενος — grieved/sad. "Corazón roto" is an invented escalation,
not a translation or a reasonable inference.)
✅ "...y se fue triste." — matches what the text and the Greek word actually say.

❌ Inventing that a character trembled, wept, or felt a specific named
emotion the text never mentions.
✅ Naming only the emotion or reaction the text states, and exploring its
theological weight — the Greek word study cards already exist for this
(e.g. λυπούμενος gets its full weight in the greek_exegesis card without
needing to be dramatized elsewhere).

**Self-check before showing ANY card to Giovanni:** for every descriptive
or emotional claim about a person, scene, or reaction, ask: does the text
say this, or does the original language support this specific word? If the
honest answer is "no, I added this for effect," rewrite it using only what
the text states. When in doubt, use the plainer, less dramatic word — the
theology carries the weight, not the adjective.

---

## MANDATORY GATE — NO ABSTRACT, UNANCHORED PHRASES (check before every card, every time)

**This is a hard gate, not a style preference.** A sentence can pass every
other gate — nothing invented, no negation-built argument, no verse text
duplicated — and still fail because it sounds profound but says nothing
concrete or verifiable. Caught repeatedly during review of
bread_of_life_001 (2026-07-14): Giovanni's diagnostic question each time
was simply "¿qué quiere decir esto exactamente?" — if the answer requires
inferring unstated context or decoding jargon, the sentence fails.

**Self-check before writing ANY sentence in `content` or `revelation_key`:**
ask "if a reader asked 'what does this actually mean, concretely?', could I
answer without adding new information?" If the honest answer only restates
the same sentence more poetically, or leans on unexplained theological
shorthand (tipología, sombra, cumplimiento) without unpacking it in plain
words, rewrite it to name the concrete fact, event, or action it's pointing
to.

❌ "El que un día se llamará a sí mismo Pan de vida primero practicó la
verdad que después proclamaría." (Sounds elegant, says nothing concrete —
practiced *what*, *how*?)
✅ "Jesús mismo, antes de decir 'Yo soy el pan de vida', vivió esa misma
verdad: en el desierto, con hambre real, el diablo lo tentó a convertir
piedras en pan. Jesús no lo hizo." — names the actual event.

❌ "cantaba sobre el maná... sin saber que cantaba sobre una sombra."
("Sombra" is unexplained typology jargon — a plain reader has no idea what
it means.)
✅ "cantaba sobre el maná... No sabía que ese pan del desierto solo era un
anuncio de algo más grande que vendría después: Jesús mismo." — unpacks the
idea in plain words.

❌ "Jesús se ofrece a sí mismo, para ser recibido, no solo admirado."
(Assumes a contrast — admiration vs. reception — the text never sets up;
nobody in the passage asked Jesus for admiration.)
✅ "Jesús pide algo concreto: que vengamos a él cada día, no una sola vez."
— states what the text actually shows Jesus asking for.

**Why this is separate from the accessibility rule below:** accessibility
catches jargon and long sentences. This gate catches sentences that are
already short and plain in vocabulary, yet still empty — the failure is
conceptual, not lexical.

## WORKFLOW

### Step 1 — Extract from the Gemini chat

Read the full chat and extract:

| Field | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Central theme** | The theological concept being explored |
| **Key passages** | Every verse cited or quoted |
| **Greek/Hebrew words** | Any original language terms mentioned |
| **Structure clues** | Any outline, keys, or framework Gemini proposed |
| **Application insights** | Practical "so what" points |
| **Emotional register** | Convicting? Comforting? Awe-inspiring? Confrontational? |
| **The person behind it** | Who needs this study? What are they going through? |

**CRITICAL:** Do NOT assume the Gemini structure maps 1:1 to cards.
Gemini may give you 4 Greek words — that does not mean 4 Greek cards.
Treat everything as ore. Your job is to find the shape of the study inside it.

### Step 3 — Generate the ES JSON

Produce the full JSON file. **Enrichment is mandatory** — see rules below.

**File naming:** `[english_concept_slug]_es_001.json`
**Output path:** `discovery/es/[english_concept_slug]_es_001.json`

The filename slug is **always English**, derived from the core theological
concept. Lowercase, underscores, no accents.

✅ `union_with_christ_es_001.json`
✅ `logos_creation_es_001.json`
❌ `union_con_cristo_es_001.json` — Spanish, wrong
❌ `la_geografia_de_la_fe_es_001.json` — Spanish title, wrong

The `id` field inside the JSON matches the slug without `_es` and `.json`:
→ `"id": "union_with_christ_001"`

#### JSON Top-Level Structure

```json
{
  "id": "english_slug_001",
  "type": "discovery",
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "title": "...",
  "subtitle": "...",
  "language": "es",
  "version": "RVR1960",
  "estimated_reading_minutes": N,
  "key_verse": {
    "reference": "Libro C:V",
    "text": "RVR1960 verse text"
  },
  "cards": [...],
  "tags": [...],
  "metadata": {
    "total_word_count": N,
    "greek_words_count": N,
    "scripture_references_count": N,
    "difficulty_level": "intermediate",
    "themes": [...]
  }
}
```

#### Card Base Structure

Every card has at minimum:
```json
{
  "order": N,
  "type": "card_type",
  "icon": "emoji",
  "title": "...",
  "subtitle": "...",
  "content": "...",
  "revelation_key": "One closing sentence that lands the insight personally."
}
```

Optional card fields — add when the content earns them:
- `greek_words[]` → `{word, transliteration, meaning, revelation}`. `word` is the
  original Greek/Hebrew script; `transliteration` is always Latin-alphabet (e.g.
  `monogenēs`, not a phonetic respelling into the study's own language/script) —
  translators must leave both fields unchanged (see discovery-translator-SKILL.md).
- `scripture_references[]` → `{reference, text}` (RVR1960)
- `identity_statement` → string (who the reader IS in Christ)
- `comfort_promise` → string
- `solemn_warning` → string

#### The discovery_activation Card (ALWAYS last)

```json
{
  "order": N,
  "type": "discovery_activation",
  "icon": "🙏",
  "title": "Descubrimiento personal",
  "discovery_questions": [
    { "category": "...", "question": "..." },
    { "category": "...", "question": "..." },
    { "category": "...", "question": "..." }
  ],
  "prayer": {
    "title": "Oración de ...",
    "content": "Full prayer. Personal, warm, Scripture-grounded. Speaks TO God, not about Him."
  }
}
```

Questions must flow from the study's cards — one per major theme.
The prayer must feel like a real person praying, not a template being filled.

## CARD ARCHITECTURE RULES

### Rule — Greek/Hebrew is a tool, not the structure
The Gemini chat may mention multiple Greek or Hebrew words. That does not mean
one Greek card per word. Ask: does this word genuinely require the original
term to understand? If yes — one card, well explained. If no — weave the
insight into a theological or application card without the Greek label.

**Maximum one greek_exegesis or hebrew_exegesis card per study** unless the
study is explicitly a word study. Even then, discuss with Giovanni first.

### Rule — Natural card rhythm
Look at existing studies. The rhythm is:
- Open with something that draws the reader in (context, contrast, mystery)
- Go deep in the middle (theology, exegesis, OT connection, typology)
- Land personally (application, identity, transformation)
- Close with activation (questions + prayer)

Do not stack the same card type. Do not put all the Greek at the top.
Do not put all the application at the bottom. Let the study breathe.

### Rule — 5–7 cards is the sweet spot
- 5 cards: tight, focused, one clear thread
- 6 cards: most common, allows one extra depth layer
- 7 cards: only for studies with multiple significant OT/NT threads
- 8+ cards: discuss with Giovanni before going here

## ENRICHMENT RULES (mandatory)

The finished study must be **significantly richer** than the Gemini input.

### Rule — Every passage needs context
Don't quote a verse and move on. Add:
- Who is speaking, to whom, and why
- What the original audience understood that we easily miss
- What changes when you know the context

### Rule — Theological tension before resolution
Don't rush to the application. Surface the tension first:
- What is hard or surprising about this text?
- What does it cost to believe this?
- What common misreading does this correct?

### Rule — Greek/Hebrew cards must go all the way
When you do use a greek_exegesis card:
- Etymology and real semantic range (what does the word actually mean?)
- What false reading or shallow understanding it corrects
- Contrast with a related word the author could have used but didn't
- A `revelation_key` that lands the implication personally

### Rule — Content formatting (match existing studies)
- Section headers: ALLCAPS with emoji prefix → `🔍 LO QUE PABLO HACE:`
- Parallel points: bullet with `•`
- Sequential arguments: `1️⃣ 2️⃣ 3️⃣`
- Short paragraphs — no walls of text
- Bold key words when they need to stand out: `**LOGOS**`

### Rule — Minimum content per card
- Opening card: 250–400 words
- Body cards: 300–500 words each
- discovery_activation: prayer 100–180 words; each question 30–60 words

## CARD TYPE VOCABULARY

| Type | Use when |
|---|---|
| `opening_mystery` | A surprising detail, paradox, or question that earns the reader's attention |
| `contrast_analysis` | The meaning emerges from comparing two opposites (En Adán vs En Cristo) |
| `historical_context` | The historical background is essential to understand the passage |
| `cultural_context` | First-century customs, geography, or social context unlocks the meaning |
| `character_context` | Understanding who the person is (background, failures, role) is the key |
| `greek_exegesis` | A Greek word is the hinge — and the original term genuinely matters |
| `hebrew_exegesis` | An OT Hebrew word or name carries the theological weight |
| `structural_analysis` | The literary structure of the passage itself IS the revelation |
| `typology_thread` | An OT event/person is a type (typos) pointing to Christ or NT reality |
| `typology_connection` | A single typological link between OT and NT |
| `new_testament_fulfillment` | How Jesus or the NT explicitly completes an OT promise or pattern |
| `prophetic_thread` | A prophecy traced across multiple passages to its fulfillment |
| `theological_depth` | A doctrine or concept requires careful, accessible unpacking |
| `theological_tension` | Two truths that seem to clash — hold them before resolving |
| `personal_application` | Direct, concrete application to the reader's everyday life |
| `modern_application` | How an ancient truth maps to a specific situation today |
| `identity_transformation` | The passage redefines who the reader is in Christ |
| `comfort_promise` | The passage is primarily assurance — God speaking tenderness |
| `solemn_warning` | The passage carries a serious, unavoidable warning |

## LANGUAGE RULES (non-negotiable)

### No marketing clichés
These phrases are forbidden everywhere — titles, subtitles, content,
revelation_key, prayers:

❌ "que lo cambia todo" / "that changes everything"
❌ "que transforma tu vida"
❌ "revolucionario" / "revolutionary"
❌ "poderoso" used as empty filler
❌ "increíble" / "amazing" / "impactante"
❌ "descubre el secreto de..."
❌ "la clave que nunca te enseñaron"
❌ Anything that reads like a YouTube thumbnail or a book cover blurb

The theology carries its own weight. State it plainly and let it land.

### Accessible to every audience
The study must be understandable by a 70-year-old grandmother with no seminary
training AND satisfying to a theologian. Both at the same time.

- Introduce Greek/Hebrew words in plain language FIRST, then go deeper
- Define theological jargon inline when you use it (typology, exegesis,
  covenant, propitiation — explain simply in the same sentence)
- One idea per sentence when possible
- Everyday analogies are encouraged when they clarify a hard concept
- Depth and clarity are not opposites

## QUALITY BAR

Before finalizing, ask:
> "Could the person who needs this most — the one who downloaded this app
> at 11pm, alone, searching — read this and encounter God in His Word?"

If the answer is no, go deeper. Add context, tension, or warmth until it is yes.

The Word does not return void. Write accordingly.
